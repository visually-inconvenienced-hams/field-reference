US band privileges

Amateur rules before transmit

Confirm the control operator, license class, authorized band, mode, power and location restrictions. Keep the entire occupied signal within the permitted segment.

Identify at least every 10 minutes during a communication and at its end. Tactical identifiers do not replace your FCC call sign.

Use the minimum necessary power. Avoid harmful interference. Messages must not be encoded to obscure their meaning, subject to the specific rule exceptions.

Amateur authority does not grant routine access to public-safety, marine, aviation, CAP, MARS or SHARES channels.

References: FCC rules

How to read the band pages

Scope: FCC-regulated stations in the contiguous United States, ITU Region 2. Do not apply these pages unchanged in other countries or regions.

All HF numbers are MHz unless marked kHz. General, Advanced and Extra are separate license classes. Technician and legacy Novice have limited HF privileges.

CW may generally be used throughout a segment authorized to the operator. Data segments here mean RTTY and data; phone segments mean phone and image, with CW also permitted.

The listed endpoints are legal boundaries, not safe dial settings. Allow room for sidebands, drift and keying bandwidth.

References: FCC rules; Band privileges

160 and 80 meter data

160 meters: 1.800-2.000. General, Advanced and Extra: CW, phone, image and data. Usual SSB convention is LSB.

80 meters CW and data: Extra 3.500-3.600. Advanced and General 3.525-3.600.

Technician and Novice: 3.525-3.600 CW only, maximum 200 W PEP output.

The 75-meter phone privileges are on the next page. Most 80-meter regional voice work uses LSB.

General maximum is 1500 W PEP output where no lower limit applies. Always use only the power needed.

60 meters continuous segment

Effective 2026-02-13: General, Advanced and Extra may use 5.3515-5.3665 MHz on a secondary basis.

Maximum radiated power is 9.15 W ERP, equivalent to 15 W EIRP. Maximum occupied bandwidth is 2.8 kHz. ERP is not simply the transmitter power setting.

The former channel centered on 5.3585 MHz is now inside this lower-power segment. Its old 100 W ERP limit no longer applies.

Protect primary users. Check antenna gain and all signal edges. USB is the usual voice convention. No Technician or Novice privilege.

References: FCC rules; 60 meters

2200 and 630 meters

General, Advanced and Extra only. 2200 meters: 135.7-137.8 kHz; maximum 1 W EIRP and 1500 W PEP transmitter output.

630 meters: 472-479 kHz; maximum 5 W EIRP and 500 W PEP transmitter output. In Alaska within 800 km of Russia, the EIRP limit is 1 W.

Notify the Utilities Technology Council for the intended location and band. Wait 30 days and ensure no objection before transmitting. Follow PLC separation and antenna restrictions in 97.303(g).

These electrically short antennas can have high voltage. Use a qualified design and keep people clear.
